SLP Instructions

Based on guidance form ASHA and CSHA you may be able to claim for assessment components in combination. Options 1, 2 and 4 below by themselves are billable and options 2 & 3 when done in combination are also billable services, whereas option 3 Evaluation of Language Comprehension and Expression by itself is not a billable service. ASHA determined the need to combine option 2 and 3 below into one procedure as their research lead them to see that in most cases options 2 & 3 are done together.

ASHA and CSHA guidance has since provided further guidance that option 2 Evaluation of Speech Sound Production, when done with Option 3 does not require a formal testing method and can be left to clinical judgment. Please see the comments and suggestions below from Shellie Badder, (CSHA) the 2016 DHCS State Training.

SLP’s usually do not conduct a language only evaluation, with no mention of a child’s speech and sound production. Even if the student has normal sound production, it should be noted in the report. Under ASHA’s guidance, language comprehension and expression may include an informal evaluation of speech sound production. The SLP’s clinical judgement to determine speech capabilities is allowable, although it must be documented.

Here are some note samples provided by Shellie Bader to cover the explanation above. (these are not to be considered compliant in all applications)

Speech Sound Production

  • The structure and function of the speech mechanism appears adequate for speech production purposes. Based on observation in multiple communication environments, speech sound production is age-appropriate when talking to peers and adults. The student’s intelligibility is judged to be at 95% in both known and unknown contexts.
  • Based on observation and informal measures, speech sound production is an area of concern. See below for further assessment results.

Speech Fluency

  • Based on observation in multiple communication environments, there are no atypical dysfluent speech patterns in conversation. Fluency is not an area of concern at this time.
  • Based on observation and informal measures, fluency is an area of concern. See below for further assessment results.

Voice and Resonance

  • Based on observation in multiple communication environments, there are no atypical voice/resonance (quality, pitch, intensity) characteristics noted in the student’s speech. Voice and resonance are not areas of concern at this time.
  • Based on observation and informal measures, voice and resonance is an area of concern. See below for further assessment results.

Language Comprehension and Expression

  • Based on informal language sampling, observation with peers and adults, and teacher/parent input, the student exhibits receptive and expressive language skills which are consistent with peers and which enable him/her to understand and use language adequately in the classroom. Language comprehension and expression are not areas of concern at this time.
  • Based on observation and informal measures, language comprehension and expression is an area of concern. See below for further assessment results.
